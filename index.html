01The show floor

A booth gets you noticed. The table is what gets remembered.

Space, build, freight, drayage, flights, and the samples shipped home again. A trade show is the most expensive attention in this industry, and it ends Friday afternoon.

What decides whether anyone follows up is the thing they picked up while they were standing there. The swatch. The box it came in. The sheet they took back to the office and put on a desk.

The booth Three days

Rented attention. It ends Friday and the samples go home in the cases they arrived in.

What's on it Every day after

A brand, a box, a data sheet and a swatch book keep working in every email, every distributor meeting and every show after this one.

Keep exhibiting. We will help make the stand look American too, and a designed booth costs about the same to build as an undesigned one. But the budget that changes your year is the one that goes into what you hand people.

02The buyer

What the American buyer wants is knowable.

The first question is which buyer. A distributor and an end user want different things from the same roll, and most manufacturers arriving here have not chosen between them. Answer that and everything downstream gets easier. Skip it and you are aimed at nobody.

A

Through distribution

  • Margin that survives two hands
  • A line that sells itself off a shelf
  • Sample support their reps can carry
  • A data sheet they can forward without rewriting
  • Confidence you will not undercut them direct
B

Direct to market

  • Settings that work on the printer in their shop
  • A brand an installer is willing to try
  • Packaging that survives a freight carrier
  • Someone in this country to call
  • Short rolls, not just full masters

Both of them read the same data sheet, and both of them read it in about four seconds.
